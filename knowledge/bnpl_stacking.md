# BNPL stacking (educational, not advice)

Buy now, pay later (BNPL) lets a shopper split one purchase into installments, often with $0 interest if every payment is on time. Stacking means holding several of those plans at once — Klarna, Afterpay, Affirm, PayPal Pay in 4, Zip, or store cards — so due dates overlap.

Why overlap is the risk:
- Each plan looks small (for example $18.75 every two weeks).
- Four plans due in the same payday window become $75, plus rent, plus subscriptions.
- Missed BNPL payments can add late fees, pause the shopper from new plans, and in some products report to credit bureaus.

AfterMath treats stacking as a calendar and cash-flow problem, not a credit-approval problem. It extracts obligations from documents the user provides, totals what hits in 7 and 30 days, and simulates extra payments. It does not recommend new borrowing or tell anyone they "can afford" a purchase.
